# Standing lessons

- Don't take a post-earnings gap (held or faded) on the screen's numbers alone; name what will carry the reaction forward, and check what the reaction was about (a deal, a split, departures) rather than the beat or miss. The mechanical 11-09 gap trades went 3 for 9, mean about -307 bp (ABNB, CVS, U, RDW, UBER and CGNX lost; OUST, QCOM and NVO won).
- Don't take a stretched name with no turn as a bounce or a fade unless a dated event inside the window can turn it: the 93 the passes left alone, scored in the screen's direction, averaged -92 bp with 42% winners (to 2026-11-23). It was -132 bp on 70 to 11-20, so the 23 that finished since averaged about +30 bp: the one falling tape behind this lesson broke on 11-23 (S&P 500 +4.7%), so recheck it over the next week before leaning on it.
- Don't short a breakdown that has no story of its own (the name fell with its sector or the tape): the 28 breakdowns the passes left alone, scored as shorts, averaged -286 bp with 32% winners (to 2026-11-23; -402 bp on 13 to 11-20). The breakdown shorts worth taking had a company or macro reason that was still running.
- Don't buy a reported takeover after the first-day pop unless a price has been reported or can be worked out and the stock is well under it: BILL (-279 bp, agreed at the rumor-day price) and ZS (-62 bp, agreed about 3% above the entry, in acquirer stock that fell 9% on the news) both paid about the deal price and kept only the break risk (two trades, to 2026-11-23).
