# Standing lessons

- Don't take a post-earnings gap (held or faded) on the screen's numbers alone; name what will carry the reaction forward, and check what the reaction was about (a deal, a split, departures) rather than the beat or miss. The mechanical 11-09 gap trades went 3 for 9, mean about -307 bp (ABNB, CVS, U, RDW, UBER and CGNX lost; OUST, QCOM and NVO won).
- Don't take a stretched name with no turn as a bounce or a fade unless a dated event inside the window can turn it: the 113 the passes left alone, scored in the screen's direction, averaged -69 bp with 46% winners, and the 26 stretched baskets -162 bp with 27% (settled scoring, to 2026-11-24). It was -132 bp on 70 to 11-20 and has eased since the tape turned on 11-23 (S&P 500 +4.7%), but it is still negative, so the lesson stands at lower weight.
- Don't pass a breakdown short only because it has no story of its own; weigh what a market bounce does to it instead: once ideas are counted only after their time limit (settled scoring), the 38 breakdowns the passes left alone, scored as shorts, averaged +189 bp with 71% winners and the 4 basket breakdowns +553 bp (to 2026-11-24). The earlier -286 bp on 28 counted early stop-outs ahead of the survivors. It is one falling month, and the two fresh shorts that lost (INTU, MTZ) were entered overnight after a washout and stopped at the next open; the three settled breakdown takes averaged +745 bp with one loser (MTZ).
- Don't buy a reported takeover after the first-day pop unless a price has been reported or can be worked out and the stock is well under it: BILL (-279 bp, agreed at the rumor-day price) and ZS (-62 bp, agreed about 3% above the entry, in acquirer stock that fell 9% on the news) both paid about the deal price and kept only the break risk (two trades, to 2026-11-23).
