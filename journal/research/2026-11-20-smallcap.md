# Small-cap pass for the 2026-11-20 session

A shadow run: verdicts are recorded and scored later, nothing is traded. The screen ran at 11:34 PM CT on Thursday, November 19, on that day's closing prices, about five hours after the 6:30 PM CT target. It gave 10 candidates from a universe of 425: one earnings reaction and nine insider-buying names.

## The tape

Thursday closed hard risk-off. IWM (the small-cap ETF) fell 2.18% to 264.22, SPY (the S&P 500 ETF) 1.96% to 715.87, QQQ (the Nasdaq 100 ETF) 2.19% to 645.04, and the VIX rose 15.8% to 28.44. The 10-year yield is 5.05%. The reported backdrop is Fed minutes that left a December hike open, which hits small caps hardest because they borrow more and often at floating rates. One lesser-known source ties the afternoon reversal to selling after a large AI listing; no second source confirmed that. Small-cap decliners mostly tracked the index with no news of their own. Overnight, crude is steady at $82.85 and Yahoo shows gold futures down about 4.3% to about $3,528.

With that tape I favoured names that have their own reason to move (senior insiders buying after a reset, and a stock that held up on a down day) over levered, rate-sensitive ones.

## Takes

1. **HMH (HMH Holding), long, conviction 3, up to 5 sessions, 10% stop.** The CFO bought about $1.65M and the CEO $88K two weeks after a Q3 miss and guidance cut took the stock down 38%. Orders beat revenue (book-to-bill 1.12), management calls the Middle East shortfall deferral, and the stock rose 1.4% on a day IWM fell 2.2%. Wrong if Baker Hughes or Akastor starts selling the 31.9M shares registered for resale, or the stock closes below the November low near $10.31.
2. **OFRM (Once Upon a Farm), long, conviction 3, up to 4 sessions, 10% stop.** A toddler-bar recall and guidance cut took it from about $16 to $9. Four insiders bought this week, it has no debt and $106.4M of cash against about $30M of recall cost, and it rose 8.9% on Thursday. Wrong if injuries, an FDA escalation, a second recall or a retailer dropping the brand appears, or it closes below $11.30.
3. **NPCE (NeuroPace), long, conviction 2, up to 5 sessions, 10% stop.** The CEO, CFO and a director each bought about $100K days after a Q3 miss cut the stock from $13 to $8. Management says Q4 cases have started to recover, and the stock fell only 1.0% on Thursday. Thin, at $1.7M a day. Wrong if the company files a share sale, discloses covenant trouble, or the stock closes below $7.50.
