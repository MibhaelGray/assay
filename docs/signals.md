# Signals, screen and dashboard

What the daily bars are turned into, how the screen picks candidates, the themed baskets, and the dashboard. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## Forced flows

The brief's `forced_flows` section estimates trading that has to happen regardless of anyone's view, from the stored daily bars:
- **Calendar:** monthly and quarterly option expiries, VIX expiry, month and quarter ends, in sessions from tonight.
- **Leveraged ETFs:** the close-of-day hedge of 18 leveraged and inverse funds, grouped by index: net assets × L(L−1) × the index return. Net assets come from IBKR into `journal/reference/leveraged_assets.json` (refreshed weekly) and are scaled by each fund's close.
- **Month- and quarter-end rebalancing:** drift of a 60/40 SPY/AGG portfolio, ranked against completed periods.
- **Vol-control funds:** exposure of a 10% vol-target fund on SPY (EWMA vol), and how a −3% to +2% session would change it.
- **Trend followers:** a 20/60/120/250-day trend score per asset and the price where each lookback flips in the next session.

The leveraged funds and AGG are pulled as daily bars only; they aren't trade legs (`universe.idea_universe()`). The dashboard's Market page shows the calendar, the leveraged funds, vol control and trend.

## The screen and the dashboard

At 7:00 PM ET the nightly job builds the brief, refreshes earnings dates and earnings-gap history, and runs the scan and the screen. The screen (`fr screen`) checks every stock, ETF, basket and factor pair against the setups in `journal.SETUPS` (about 1,240 names since 2026-11-18; see "The universe" below) and keeps the best 40 (20 until 2026-11-16), each with what triggered it, default legs and a default plan. Every stock and ETF candidate also carries two chart measures: how tight its daily ranges were over the 10 sessions before the signal against the 50 before, and whether its trend is up, down or mixed (the 50-day average against the 200-day, and its direction over 20 sessions). They move the ranking only for breakouts: a tight base adds a little and a breakout inside a downtrend loses a little more (`TIGHT_BASE` and `ADJUST` in `screen.py`, with the five-year check behind them). The shortlist is built so its slots hold different ideas: a name the paper book holds or closed in the last three sessions is left out (and listed under `in_book`), no setup takes more than ten slots, funds that gave the same signal and move together count once, and each stock's default hedge is the market, sector or industry fund it has moved with most over 60 sessions (`hedge_fit`; `weak` when nothing moves with it). That shortlist is all a screen decides. The dashboard shows the nightly one; each research pass builds the same screen on live prices (`fr screen --live`), judges each candidate, and only its takes are traded (see the swing book in [operations.md](operations.md#data-and-jobs)).

A trade's side is one symbol or a basket of stocks in equal weight. A pair is dollar-neutral; an outright long or short carries the market's move. A leg can be any US-listed stock or ETF, not only the standard universe: `fr bars` adds the paper book's legs and every basket member to its pull, and the nightly job runs `fr bars --pending` in case a proposal arrived after the 4:30 PM pull. Indexes such as SPX or VIX can't be legs.

**The universe** (`universe.py`, widened 2026-11-12) has two tiers. The core is the original 46 ETFs and the 110 largest stocks: the brief's stock technicals stay on it. The swing tier adds 18 industry ETFs (`MORE_INDUSTRIES`: SOXX, XSD, CIBR, IBB, IHI, KBE, KIE, IYT, JETS, XHB, PAVE, XAR, OIH, XME, COPX, GDX, URA, TAN) and the rest of the S&P 500 and S&P 400 that trades at least $20M a day (`INDEX_STOCKS`, 741 names in `src/factor_rotation/index_stocks.json` with each one's index, GICS sector and sub-industry; refresh it when the indexes reconstitute). Since 2026-11-18 it also takes 182 big foreign companies listed in the US that neither index holds, such as TSMC, Shopify, Spotify, ARM, Novo Nordisk and MercadoLibre (`FOREIGN_STOCKS`, `src/factor_rotation/foreign_stocks.json`, same $20M floor, with each one's country, sector and industry from Yahoo). TSM joined the processor chips basket the same day (cohesion 0.58); it fit none of the baskets hedged with SMH, which it tracks closely. The screen, the nightly bars, earnings dates and the hedge choices (`fr paper hedges`, the screen's default hedge) cover both tiers, and four factor pairs use the new funds (transports, gold miners against gold, small against large biotech, metals). Yahoo refuses earnings-date calls after about 500 in quick succession, so `fr earnings` pulls the core names every night and the wider tier 150 a night, names reporting within 3 days before or 10 after today first, then the stalest (`WIDE_PER_NIGHT` in `earnings.py`); a name that fails keeps its last dates.

`journal/reference/baskets.json` holds a watchlist of themed baskets, each with its hedge ETF, the story behind it, a reason for every member and a snapshot of the day's moves from IBKR. `fr bars` pulls daily bars for every member, `fr market` adds the baskets' spread against their hedge to the market rows, and the dashboard shows them on its Baskets page (`#baskets`). The watchlist isn't scored; a basket is scored only when a proposal trades it. Since 2026-11-16 every basket must pass `fr baskets check` (see the job table in [operations.md](operations.md#data-and-jobs)): a group that only moves with its sector fund, or a member that doesn't move with the rest, is cut. The list holds 104 baskets: 36 themed ones (among them Genomics and diagnostics and App and game advertising, added that night) and 68 industry baskets that sort the S&P 500 by GICS sub-industry, with S&P 400 peers where a group needed a third name. 416 of the index's 503 tickers are in a basket. The other 87 failed the test in every sensible group (mega-caps such as Apple, Nvidia, Exxon and Walmart that dominate their own sector fund, the beverage and tobacco names, and one-off businesses) and are screened as single stocks only; nothing is forced in. Basket members are screened like any other name. The Baskets page has two views: a sortable table, and a heat map that draws every basket as one block of a treemap, each member a tile sized by the square root of its 90-day dollar volume and coloured by its move today, over 5 days or over 20 days; click a basket's name for its story and members.

The dashboard's Chart page (`#chart=NVDA`) only works under `fr serve`, because the claude.ai page can't pull live data. Type a ticker, or click one in Baskets or Earnings, to see its last 60 days of 15-minute bars from Yahoo with daily and 15-minute averages, RSI, ATR and the nightly brief's technicals. It covers the names on the dashboard, pulls when opened, and saves nothing. Indicators on 15-minute bars use the regular session only. Yahoo reports no pre-market or after-hours volume, and its highs and lows there can be bad prints, so those candles show only the open and close. The chart loads Lightweight Charts from a CDN.

The dashboard's Earnings page (`#earnings`) lists who reports in the next ten sessions and how reports from the last five traded. Reactions come from our daily bars: the gap and the day on the first session that traded on the report, alone and against the name's basket hedge. Some Yahoo dates are estimates until the company confirms them.

## Portfolio view

The dashboard's Portfolio page (`#portfolio`) shows the swing book the way a broker does, in four parts. A summary strip gives the account's value, the book's profit, the change since the last close and net exposure as a long-against-short bar; the other figures (returns three ways, money at work, commissions, the pause level, the unassigned remainder) sit in a fold-out table under it. The profit-and-loss chart shows the S&P 500's change over the same time, with 1D, 1W, 1M, MTD, 3M, YTD and All. Positions are listed by trade (one row per open trade with its exposure, today's change, total, result and room to its stop; click a row for its legs, plan and reasoning) or by symbol, with filters for long, short, winners and losers and sorting on every column; tickers open the Chart page. A last panel is the closed-trade history (latest session, the week, or everything), where a trade opens to its legs, plan and reasoning.

Portfolio is the only page that shows money: positions, the profit-and-loss chart and every closed trade. The Swing research page (`#paper`) holds the decision record instead: the verdicts, the reasoning, and how takes did against passes. A trade on Portfolio links to its verdict, and a taken verdict links back to its trade. `portfolio.py` builds it from the book's fills: positions by symbol at average cost, marked at the latest daily bar, and the running profit and loss after commissions at each session's close. Under `fr serve`, 1D and 1W use 15-minute Yahoo bars (`/api/portfolio/pnl?book=`). The published copy has the daily frames only.

The swing book has $1,000,000 of capital (`journal/paper/book.json`, since the restart of 2026-11-15) and is kept as a sub-account of the one IBKR paper account (`portfolio.accounts()`): its value is its capital plus the realized and open profit and loss from its own fills, after commissions. Open positions are marked at IBKR's own prices from the last sync (`marks` in `journal/paper/fills/<session>.json`, the prices the account's value is struck at), falling back to the latest daily close. What the account is worth beyond the book is shown as unassigned: the account's value before the book began plus interest and borrow fees. It should move slowly; a jump means a position or cost the book doesn't account for. "Today" is the book's profit and loss at IBKR's prices less what it was at the close before the latest session (`day_pnl` in `portfolio.rows()`); the chart's 1D figure uses Yahoo's 15-minute prices, so the two can differ by a few dollars. The book's return is shown on its own capital and on the whole account's value. A third return is on capital deployed (`portfolio.deployed()`): the book's gross exposure at cost, longs plus shorts, after every fill, averaged over calendar time since its first fill, so idle hours count against it. The fold-out table shows that return with the average, current and peak amount deployed.
