          How the baskets are built

          Members come from IBKR's theme lists and the night's research, and each one is checked first: a US listing and at least about $20M a day of average trading over 90 days. Today's moves come from daily bars, or the stored snapshot before bars exist. The 5- and 20-day spread and its z-score come from daily bars, rebalanced to equal weight each day, and appear once every member has bars.

          The hedge is one dollar against one dollar, with no beta adjustment, so baskets that swing more than their ETF (quantum, neoclouds, space) partly measure the market's direction. Members are chosen for today's story, so their past spreads look cleaner than anything tradable at the time.

          How the swing book works

          The swing book trades on an IBKR paper account, never a live one. It was started again from zero with $1,000,000, and it is the only book. Three times a day a full research pass runs: at 7:30 PM CT (Sunday to Thursday, for the next session), 8:45 AM CT and 12:00 PM CT. Each pass starts from a screen of about 1,040 stocks and funds on the latest prices, whether after-hours, pre-market or the regular session. Research agents study each of about 40 shortlisted names, and the pass writes a verdict on every one: take or pass, a conviction from 1 to 5, a rank and its reasoning.

          Every take of a pass is traded, strongest conviction first until the book's capital is in use, as soon as the pass records, usually within a minute. Every order hits IBKR’s own bid or ask, and whatever it doesn’t fill at once is cancelled, so nothing is left resting. With no quote, or a spread wider than 2% on any leg, nothing is sent for that trade, and the order job tries again every 15 minutes until 2:40 PM CT. A trade's hardest-to-fill leg goes first and its other legs only follow what that leg has filled, so a stuck order never leaves a hedge on its own. Each take carries its own plan: a time limit of up to 10 sessions and a stop between 1% and 8%. Its size comes from that stop and its conviction: a trade loses about 0.15% of the book if stopped at conviction 3, up to twice that at conviction 5, so a wild stock gets fewer dollars than a calm one. Every pass also reviews each open trade and can close one early for a stated reason, or ask for a winner on its last session to run up to 3 sessions longer, once. It can never widen a stop or hold a loser past its time limit.

          At 2:45 PM CT every open trade is checked against its stop and time limit, and the ones leaving are sent as market-on-close orders. Every verdict, taken or passed, is scored from the prices at the moment its pass recorded, so the passes are the control for the takes. The book pauses if it falls $100,000 below its high. The first book’s record, and the intraday book’s, are kept in the journal’s archive.

          How the chart is built

          Prices come from Yahoo Finance when you open a name, and nothing is saved. Pre-market and after-hours bars show as gray candles on shaded bands with only their open and close, since Yahoo gives no volume there and its highs and lows can be bad prints. The price scale fits the candles, so an average far from price can sit off the chart; its value stays in the key. RSI 14, ATR 14 and the dashed averages use regular-session 15-minute bars, so a 200-bar average covers about 15 sessions. The solid lines are the 20, 50 and 200-day averages on dividend-adjusted prices, and the daily figures on the right match the nightly brief with today's session so far included.
